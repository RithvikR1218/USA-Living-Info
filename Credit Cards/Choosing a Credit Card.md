Selecting the right credit card boils down to aligning a card's reward structure with your actual spending habits rather than chasing shiny advertised perks.

# Step 1:
Before looking at specific cards, choose your primary reward strategy and reference [[Miles vs Cash Back]]
# Step 2: Map Cards to Your Major Spending Categories
Credit cards offer different reward rates (e.g., 3% or 3x points) depending on the transaction category.

>[!Example: Capital One Savor Cash Rewards Card]
>- 3% cash back on Dining
>-  3% cash back on Groceries
>-  3% cash back on Entertainment & popular streaming services
>- 1% back on all other purchases

>[!Note:]
 **Building a Complementary Setup:** If your primary card covers dining and groceries (like the Savor card above), your next card should ideally fill in the gaps by targeting missing everyday expenses, such as gas stations, online shopping, or wholesale clubs.
# Step 3: Run the Math to Pick the Right Card

Instead of guessing which card gives the best return, analyze your real transaction data:
- Track Spending: Review your bank or debit card statements from the past 1–3 months.
- Categorize: Group your purchases into common buckets (e.g., Groceries, Dining, Gas, Travel, Shopping, Miscellaneous)
- Calculate Annual Yield: Multiply your estimated annual spend in each category by the card’s reward percentage
- Factor in Annual Fees & Sign-Up Bonuses (SUB):
- Compare Net Return: Pick the card that yields the highest net return based on your actual budget.

>[!Note:]
>If you have zero credit history, major banks may not approve you for mid- or high-tier rewards cards right away. Start with a student card, a basic no-annual-fee entry card, or a secured card to build 6–12 months of solid credit history before applying for specialized rewards cards
