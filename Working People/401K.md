# What
401(k) is an **employer-sponsored retirement savings plan** with special tax benefits. Benefits depend on whether it is a [[Roth vs Traditonal]] but this is something that the employer decides on. Employers typically offer 401(k)s as part of a benefits package to attract and retain workers

# Why
- The benefit of the 401(K) is that companies provide a match up to a certain percentage of your salary that goes into your 401(K) account. 
- Once you create the account, you can then have your 401(K) track a certain index fund/ETF where it can grow over time until you finally retire

# How
> [!Example]
> - Lets say Company A has a 100% match for the first 3% and and 50% match for the next 3 percent of your salary for a traditional 401(K)
> - This means when you put 3% of your salary into the 401(K), the company will also keep the exact same amount in your 401(K)
> - When you keep the next 3%, the company will keep half that amount in your account
> - This leads to an effective 4.5% match for 6% of your salary

# Important Info:

### Vesting Schedules
While money you contribute from your paycheck is always 100% yours immediately, the **employer match** may be subject to a **vesting schedule**:
* **Immediate Vesting:** You own 100% of the match right away
* **Graded Vesting:** You gain ownership of the employer match in percentage increments over several years (e.g., 20% per year over 5 years)
* **Cliff Vesting:** You zero-percent own the match until you complete a specific duration of service (e.g., 0% until year 3, then 100% at year 3)

> [!Warning]
>  If you leave the company before becoming fully vested, you forfeit any unvested portion of the employer match

### Contribution Limits & Penalties
* The IRS caps employee contributions (e.g., $24,500 for tax year 2026; employer matches do not count toward this employee cap).
* Withdrawing money before age 59½ generally incurs income tax plus a 10% penalty fee, losing the account's primary tax benefits.

### Borrowing Against Your 401(k)
* You can take a **401(k) loan** and pay interest back to yourself rather than a bank.
* **Caveat:** The loan is tied to your employer. If you leave or lose your job before repaying it, the full remaining balance typically becomes due shortly after departure—otherwise, it is treated as an early withdrawal subject to taxes and penalties.
* Its generally not recommended but its good to know about if you really need a cash investment as soon as possible
