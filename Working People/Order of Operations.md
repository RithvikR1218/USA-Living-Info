# What
An "Order of Operations" for personal finance is a prioritized strategy for allocating your income. It ensures you optimize tax savings, capture free money, build a safety net, and build long-term wealth in the most efficient sequence.

# Why
Not all accounts are created equal. By following a structured hierarchy:
* **Capture Free Money First:** You never pass up guaranteed employer contributions or stock discounts.
* **Protect Against Risk:** You build liquid reserves so emergencies don't force you into high-interest debt or early retirement withdrawals.
* **Maximize Tax Efficiency:** You fund tax-shielded accounts (401k, IRA, HSA) before putting a single dollar into taxable accounts.
* **Grow Surplus Wealth:** Any remaining cash goes into unconstrained taxable brokerage accounts for general long-term goals.


# How (The Priority Waterfall)

1) Get employee match for [[401K]]
	- Most companies will have some kind of match and its best to maximize that since its free money
2) Keep money for Emergency Fund in [[HYSA]]
	- Its recommended to have at least a 6 month emergency fund
	- This can be for health related issues, car problems etc.
	- Based on your risk tolerance, it can go anywhere from 3 to 12 months but this is upto you
3) Max out [[HSA]]
	- This is if you have an HSA with the HDHP
	- If you decide to get an HSA, its worth keeping some extra money in it for the triple tax advantages 
	- Hopefully your company also gives some seed money in your HSA
4) Put money into [[ESPP]]
	- Its up to you if you want to sell or hold but most companies give a 15% discount 
	- Just be careful of having too much money in a single account
5) Put money into [[IRA]]
	- Its best to max out the rest of your tax advantaged accounts
	- They have slightly lower yearly limits and should be easier to hit 
6) Max out 401(K)
	- The 401(K) has a much higher yearly limit of 24K and so you probably need to keep some more money in it as well
7) Put money into Brokerage account
	- Any excess money you have for investing goes into your brokerage accounts


### Student Loans:
- If you have any student loans, You need to decide at which step you want to stop and focus on the debt 
- What I am personally doing is going up to step 5
- The money that I'm putting towards the ESPP will be used for student loans since I get 15% and with tax is around 11-13 which is higher than my interest rates
- Im not planning on investing using brokerage accounts since the historical average of the S&P 500 is 10% and with inflation it comes out to 7% which is lower than my interest rate
# Important Notes
* **High-Interest Debt Exception:** If you carry high-interest debt (like credit cards with 15%+ APR), pay it off right after capturing your employer 401(k) match (Step 1) and establishing a basic 1-month cash buffer.
* **Flexibility:** Step priorities can adapt based on personal preferences (e.g., some prefer maxing an IRA before utilizing an ESPP depending on holding periods and company terms).
* **Automate the Flow:** Set up automatic W-2 payroll deductions and regular bank transfers so your money flows down this waterfall automatically every payday.
