# What
A Flexible Spending Account (FSA) is an employer-sponsored, tax-advantaged account that lets you pay for eligible out-of-pocket healthcare expenses using pre-tax dollars. By contributing directly from your paycheck before federal, state, and payroll taxes are calculated, you lower your overall taxable income and reduce out-of-pocket healthcare costs.

# Why:
Using pre-tax money for medical expenses essentially acts as an immediate discount equal to your tax bracket rate.

Beyond major procedures, FSAs cover everyday out-of-pocket costs, including:
* **Medical:** Doctor co-pays, deductibles, prescriptions, and hospital fees.
* **Dental & Vision:** Braces, crowns, eyeglasses, contact lenses, prescription sunglasses, and LASIK.
* **Over-the-Counter:** Sunscreen (SPF 15+), first-aid kits, menstrual products, and pain relievers.
* **Day-1 Availability (Uniform Coverage Rule):** Your full elected annual contribution is available on Day 1 of the plan year, even before you have paid it all in through paycheck deductions.

# How:
* **Enrollment:** You sign up during your employer's annual Open Enrollment period or following a Qualifying Life Event (e.g., marriage, birth of a child).
* **Payroll Deductions:** You choose how much to set aside, and your employer splits it evenly into pre-tax payroll deductions across the year.
* **2026 Contribution Limit:** For 2026, the IRS caps individual Health FSA contributions at **$3,400 per year** (if both you and a spouse have access to an FSA, each can contribute up to $3,400).
* **Accessing Funds:** You can pay directly with an FSA debit card or submit receipts online for reimbursement after paying out of pocket.

# Important Info:
* Unused funds are generally **forfeited** at the end of the plan year. However, employers can legally offer **one** of two options (not both):
	  1. *Carryover:* Roll over up to **$680 of unused funds** into the next plan year.
	  2. *Grace Period:* An extra **2.5 months** after the plan year ends to spend remaining funds.
* If you leave your job, you usually can't take your FSA funds with you. 
* FSAs should primarily be used for predictable expenses, such as planned surgeries (e.g., LASIK), ongoing prescription schedules, or known dental/orthodontic work.
