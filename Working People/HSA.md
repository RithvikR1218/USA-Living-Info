# What
An HSA is a tax-advantaged account that can be used to save and pay for qualified medical expenses. It also acts as an investment account where it grows overtime as well

# Why
The HSA is considered a Triple tax advantaged account:
- The money comes from your salary pre tax and you dont need to pay any kind of income, state, etc. taxes on it
- It grows tax free and no capital tax gains on its growth
- Finally, you can take money out tax free when you are using it for medical expenses.  

Many companies also provide initial seed money when opening up an HSA which is also free money 

# How
- You need to have a High Deductible Health Plan (HDHP)
- While the HDHP does have high deductible, it makes more sense to have this when you are younger and are less likely to use it
- You can then decide how much money to put into the HSA which comes from your salary pre-tax

### Account Structure (Cash vs. Investing):
- Every HSA is split into two parts under one account:
	1. **Liquid Cash Account:** Where your payroll contributions initially go. This connects to your HSA debit card to pay immediate medical bills.
	2. **Investment Account:** Where you can invest money into stocks, ETFs, or mutual funds for long-term tax-free growth.

- This is why its important to know that you can sell stocks in the account tax free and then use it for medical expenses afterwards by withdrawing it tax free as well
# Important Notes
- Unlike a 401(K), the HSA is yours and not tied to a company
- For 2026, you can contribute upto $4,300 per year
- You can use the money from an HSA for non medical expenses after you turn 65 without having to pay taxes
- Apparently you do need to pay state taxes for HSA in California and New Jersey
