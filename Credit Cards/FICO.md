# What
A FICO Score is a three-digit number used by 90% of top lenders to assess creditworthiness. It is based on factors like payment history, credit utilization, and length of credit history, helping lenders make consistent and fair decisions. 

 Your score is calculated separately using data from each of the three major credit bureaus (**Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion**). Because not all lenders report to all three bureaus, your score can vary slightly depending on which bureau's report is being pulled.
# Why
- Pretty much anything related to lending will be based on your FICO score
- Having a better score will give you more favourable lending terms such as lower interest rates or more premium credit cards
- While the perks may seem really small, having even a 1% difference in a car loan or mortgage can be the difference between thousands of dollars

# How
Here are the most important factors:
- **Payment history (35%):** Whether you've paid past credit accounts on time.
- **Credit Utilization (30%):** The amount of credit you're using compared to your total credit limit.
- **Length of credit history (15%):** How long your credit accounts have been established.
- **New credit (10%):** How often you've applied for or opened new credit accounts.
- **[Credit mix](https://www.myfico.com/credit-education/credit-scores/credit-mix) (10%):** The variety of credit accounts you have, such as credit cards, mortgages, and auto loans.

# Important Info
- It isn't worth taking out a loan such as car for the sake of opening more types of credit. If you can afford it, it isnt worth taking out the loan
- There are other scores such as VantageScore but FICO is what most lenders use so make sure to track your FICO Score in particular
- It is worth downloading the Experian app just to keep track of your credit score. Most banks also have the option to see your credit card but it might not show the FICO score though 
- To ensure you dont have issues with interest, set up **Auto Pay with Statement Balance**
- Statement Balance makes it pay the entire debt and ensures there is no debt carried over along with the interest payment

### Hard vs. Soft Inquiries
- **Soft pulls** (checking your own score, or a lender pre-approving you) **do not** affect your score. 
- **Hard pulls** (when you formally apply for a credit card, mortgage, or loan) temporarily drop your score by a few points
- _Exception:_ FICO models have a **rate-shopping buffer**. Multiple hard inquiries for an auto loan, mortgage, or student loan clustered within a short window (typically 14 to 45 days, depending on the FICO version) count as a single inquiry so you aren't penalized for shopping around for the best rates.
