# What:
These terms refer to the **tax treatment** applied to tax-advantaged retirement accounts like the [[401K]] (employer-sponsored) and the [[IRA]] (Individual Retirement Account).

If the account like an IRA is of a Roth variety, it is called a Roth IRA and the other type is called a Traditional IRA

Similarly, we have a traditional 401(K) and a Roth 401(K)

## Traditional 
- If the account is a traditional, then it is a pre-tax advantaged account. Which means that the money is taken out before taxes are applied.
- This means that you save money up front but you have to pay taxes when you sell the stock but can re-invest it into something else
- Selling stocks or re-balancing assets inside the account triggers **$0 in capital gains taxes**. Money from sales remains in cash within the account to re-invest.
-  When you pull money out in retirement (after age 59½), every dollar withdrawn is taxed as **ordinary income**.

## Roth
- If the account is a Roth, it is a post-tax advantaged account which means that you have to pay taxes initially but dont have to pay taxes later on
- This means that you have less money to work with but dont have to pay taxes on a much larger amount
- All qualified withdrawals in retirement (contributions + earnings) are **100% tax-free**.
- For a Roth IRA, you can withdraw your original contributions (principal) at any time, for any reason, penalty-free and tax-free.
